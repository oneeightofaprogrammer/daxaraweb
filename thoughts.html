02 August 2026

The mall as institution

Every institution we trust to shape people — the school, the temple, the university — was, at some point, just a building that got there first. The mall got to commerce first, and we've spent thirty years assuming that was its only possible use. But a mall is really just a piece of architecture engineered for one thing: getting a large number of unrelated people to move through the same space, repeatedly, without being told to. That's not a retail problem. That's an institutional opportunity nobody has taken seriously.

We didn't move the Academy into a mall to be provocative. We moved it there because the mall already solved the hardest problem any new institution faces — how do you get people to keep showing up before they trust you yet.

19 June 2026

On not explaining yourself

Every new venture faces a moment where it's tempting to write the paragraph that explains everything — what this is, why it matters, why now. We've written that paragraph a dozen times and thrown it away a dozen times, because it always makes Daxsara sound like something you could have guessed. The things worth building aren't fully explainable in advance. They're demonstrated, and the explanation catches up afterward, usually shorter than you expected it to need to be.

30 April 2026

Capability is not a value

When people ask what a program is 'about,' they're usually asking for a list of values dressed up as a mission statement. We resisted that for the Academy, because the thing that actually makes three months matter isn't a value — it's whether a sixteen-year-old leaves able to do something they couldn't do in week one. Capability is the substrate everything else stands on. Perception without the capability to act on it is just a more articulate form of watching.

11 February 2026

What a university cannot give back

We ran an earlier version of this work inside a university, and it taught us something a strategy memo never would have: institutional authority doesn't stay in the room. A student who has spent a decade being ranked doesn't set the ranking down at the studio door. The building carries its history into every interaction that happens inside it, whether or not that history is relevant to the work at hand. That's not a criticism of universities — it's a description of what buildings with long institutional memories actually do to the people who move through them.
